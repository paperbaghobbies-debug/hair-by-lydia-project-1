**Search visibility** - q1 - Lydia wants more people to discover her business online and make it easier for potential clients to find her and enquire. Lydia doesnt use Google Business. The website should describe her as a mobile hairdresser serving Banbury and surrounding areas, with travel. 
I should use natural wording based on her real services, such as “mobile hairdresser in Banbury” and “mobile hair colourist”. The salon SEO keyword articles provide phrase ideas but do not confirm local search volume.
I also considered how the SPA versus multi-page decision could affect search visibility. Google can render JavaScript, but that rendering happens after the initial crawl and not all crawlers run JavaScript. A multi-page site can give distinct content its own URL, title and description, while keeping important content in the delivered HTML. A single-page static site is not automatically an SPA, and neither architecture guarantees higher rankings.
 Method – I researched Google’s SEO guidance and looked at hairdresser keyword articles to consider natural wording for Lydia’s services and area, while comparing how SPA and multi-page sites can present content to search engines.
 Sources - (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics) (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) (https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) (https://www.seopital.co/blog/the-best-hair-salon-seo-keywords) (https://marketkeep.com/seo-keywords-for-hair-salons/) (https://www.ronkot.com/best-seo-keywords-for-hair-salons/) (https://digitallytop.com/digital-marketing/seo-for-hair-salons/)
