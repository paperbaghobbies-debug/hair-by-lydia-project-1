# Design Principles

**Thoughts** — To develop a research-informed approach to this project, I explored Nielsen Norman Group's design principles and Laws of UX. I used these sources to consider how layout, content, and interaction choices can make Lydia's website easier to understand and navigate.

**Sources** — ([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-principles/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-principles/))([https://lawsofux.com](https://lawsofux.com))

**Findings** — The sources describe ways that human perception and decision-making can inform design. For example, Hick's Law relates decision time to the number and complexity of choices, while the Gestalt Law of Proximity describes how nearby elements are perceived as related. Clear hierarchy and consistent interface patterns can help visitors understand what to notice and how to navigate.

**Decision** — I will organize the notes around these eight principles: visual hierarchy, consistency, contrast and colour, white space and proximity, simplicity, typography, imagery, and responsive design. I will use them to guide the layout, content, and interactions of Lydia's website.

**Accessibility target** — Use WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the working target, pending confirmation against the assignment brief. The checklist below summarizes the accessibility checks planned for the site.

## Visual hierarchy — Make the most important things stand out

**Thoughts** — Lydia wants “Enquiry” to be easy to find. I’m considering how to make that action prominent while also helping visitors understand her services and see examples of her work.

**Sources** — ([https://www.wixsolutions.co.uk/post/hair-salon-website-design](https://www.wixsolutions.co.uk/post/hair-salon-website-design))([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/))([https://www.matthewgranthairdressing.co.uk](https://www.matthewgranthairdressing.co.uk))([https://lawsofux.com](https://lawsofux.com))

**Findings** — Nielsen Norman Group describes visual hierarchy as guiding attention through elements such as contrast, scale, and grouping. The Wix guide recommends introducing the business and its services as part of the visitor journey. On Matthew Grant Hairdressing's site, I observed business and service information alongside an enquiry link near the top of the page. This is an example of one way to provide context while keeping an enquiry route visible; it does not by itself show how effective that layout is.

**Project link** — Q18 and US01/US02: Lydia wants visitors to see her work and meet “Your Personal Hair Fairy” straight away, with clear routes to enquire, view her work, and see services and prices.

**Decision** — Keep Enquiry easy to find in the site navigation and repeat it at relevant points, such as after the services and reviews. The homepage can introduce Lydia and her work before inviting visitors to enquire, without requiring the enquiry form itself to appear first.

## Consistency — Use familiar patterns consistently

**Thoughts** — To understand how consistency can guide this project, I explored Nielsen Norman Group’s usability heuristics and Jakob’s Law. I want visitors to recognise how the site works, find familiar patterns across its pages, and encounter consistent names for the same actions.

**Sources** — ([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/consistency-and-standards/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/consistency-and-standards/))([https://lawsofux.com/jakobs-law/](https://lawsofux.com/jakobs-law/))

**Findings** — Consistency applies both within the site and in relation to familiar web conventions. Within the site, repeated patterns—such as navigation, buttons, colours, and terminology—can help visitors understand how to use each page. Familiar conventions from other websites can also make interactions easier to recognise.

**Project link** — Q6, Q18, Q20, and US02: The site should feel clean, warm, and personal, use Lydia’s existing brand identity, and make the enquiry action easy to find across pages.

**Decision** — I will use consistent navigation, button styles, colour and typography choices, and wording for recurring actions across the site. The Enquiry action will remain available in the navigation on every page, and the footer will provide consistent links to Lydia's contact methods. I’ll follow familiar web conventions where they suit Lydia’s site, while adapting layouts to each page’s content and screen size.

## Contrast and colour — Use colour purposefully and maintain readable contrast

**Thoughts** — To understand how contrast and colour can support this project, I explored the W3C’s WCAG contrast guidance and Nielsen Norman Group’s visual design principles. I want the palette to create clear emphasis while keeping text and important interface elements distinguishable.

**Sources** — ([https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html))([https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html))([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/color-enhance-design/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/color-enhance-design/))

**Findings** — Colour can guide attention, but its use should support the content rather than compete with it. WCAG 2.2 requires a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. Meaningful visual information in controls and graphics has separate non-text contrast requirements. Text placed over an image needs to be checked against the part of the image behind it.

**Project link** — Q18/Q20 and US01: Build on Lydia’s warm brown, beige, and cream palette while checking text and interface contrast against the project’s accessibility requirements.

**Decision** — I will assign roles to Lydia's warm palette, such as page background, body text, borders, and accent actions. I’ll check actual foreground/background combinations, including text over images, button labels, control boundaries, and focus indicators against the relevant WCAG criteria. I’ll use more than colour alone to communicate important information. Detailed pairings and test results will be recorded in the accessibility chapter.

## White space and proximity — Use spacing to show relationships

**Thoughts** — To understand how spacing can support this project, I explored the Gestalt Law of Proximity and Nielsen Norman Group’s guidance on whitespace. I want visitors to see which pieces of information belong together and to move through each page without unnecessary visual clutter.

**Sources** — ([https://lawsofux.com/law-of-proximity/](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-proximity/))([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/whitespace-ux/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/whitespace-ux/))

**Findings** — The Law of Proximity describes how people tend to perceive nearby items as related. Whitespace can separate content groups and give important elements room to stand out. Used inconsistently, however, spacing can make relationships between items less clear.

**Project link** — Q6/Q13 and US03/US04: Keep the presentation uncluttered and make relationships clear between services and prices, reviews, and gallery images.

**Decision** — I will use spacing consistently to group related content, such as service details and review quotes with their attributions, and to separate distinct sections. I’ll apply margin, padding, and layout gaps to create these relationships and check that the spacing remains clear across screen sizes.

## Simplicity — Make key tasks straightforward

**Thoughts** — To explore how simplicity can support this project, I looked at Hick’s Law and Nielsen Norman Group’s guidance on minimalist design. I want visitors to understand their options and find relevant information without unnecessary complexity.

**Sources** — ([https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/))([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-and-minimalist-design/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-and-minimalist-design/))

**Findings** — Hick’s Law describes how decision time is influenced by the number and complexity of available choices. Nielsen Norman Group’s guidance emphasizes removing irrelevant or redundant information, while keeping content that helps users complete their tasks.

**Project link** — Q10/Q13/Q18 and US05: Make it straightforward to ask a question or get advice, while clearly explaining that an enquiry is not a confirmed booking.

**Decision** — I will keep navigation focused and avoid unnecessary menu layers or duplicate content. I’ll test that visitors can reach the enquiry form from the site-wide Enquiry link and from relevant page prompts, and that service and price information is easy to locate. I’ll retain the details visitors need to decide what is right for them.

## Typography — Choose readable type and clear hierarchy

**Thoughts** — To explore typography for this project, I reviewed Nielsen Norman Group’s guidance on legibility and readability, along with accessibility guidance on text. I want the type choices and page structure to make content easy to scan and comfortable to read across screen sizes.

**Sources** — ([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension/))([https://web-accessibility.carnegiemuseums.org/design/font/](https://web-accessibility.carnegiemuseums.org/design/font/))

**Findings** — Readability depends on more than font choice: text size, line spacing, line length, and clear heading structure all contribute. Relative units such as rem can help text scale with user settings, and concise headings and paragraphs help visitors scan for relevant information.

**Project link** — Q6/Q20 and the shared accessibility checks: Use Lydia’s preferred brand fonts where they remain readable, with a clear type hierarchy and text that scales well.

**Decision** — I will use Noto Serif Display Extra Condensed and Luxurious Script selectively to retain Lydia's existing brand identity, with a readable typeface for body copy if needed. I’ll define a consistent type scale using scalable units and check text size, line-height, line length, and resizing at 200%.

## Imagery — Use photos to show Lydia's work and home-service brand

**Thoughts** — To explore how imagery can support this project, I reviewed guidance on using photos as web content. I want visitors to see examples of Lydia's actual work and get a sense of her friendly, personalised mobile hairdressing service before making an enquiry.

**Sources** — ([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/))

**Findings** — Nielsen Norman Group’s guidance on photos as web content supports choosing relevant images that help visitors understand what a site offers. For Lydia’s site, portfolio photos should show her actual work; generic or misleading imagery may not give visitors a useful impression of her service.

**Project link** — Q13/Q21 and US01/US04: Showcase Lydia-approved photos of her actual work, including clearly identified before-and-after pairs where used.

**Decision** — I will use photos Lydia has approved and has permission to publish, with a consistent presentation that does not crop out important hairstyle details. Informative photos will have useful text alternatives; decorative images will use empty alternatives. I’ll display images responsively and optimize them for loading performance.

## Mobile-first and responsive — Design for small screens and adapt to larger screens

**Thoughts** — To explore mobile-first and responsive design, I reviewed Nielsen Norman Group’s guidance on responsive web design and mobile UX. I want visitors to be able to find key information and use the site comfortably on phones, while ensuring the layout also works on larger screens.

**Sources** — ([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/responsive-web-design-definition/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/responsive-web-design-definition/))([https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-vs-responsive/](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-vs-responsive/))

**Findings** — A mobile-first approach starts with the constraints of smaller screens and prioritises essential content and tasks. Responsive layouts adapt to different viewport sizes, but still need to be checked to ensure content remains readable and controls usable.

**Project link** — Q4/Q18 and US01: Make social visitors’ key tasks easy to complete on smaller screens and test at the specified 320, 768, and 1280 CSS-pixel widths. Q4 identifies social channels as discovery sources; it doesn’t confirm that most visitors use phones.

**Decision** — I will design the core pages for small screens first, then adapt the layouts for larger viewports. I’ll test at 320, 768, and 1280 CSS pixels for readable content, usable navigation and enquiry actions, no unintended horizontal scrolling, and support for browser zoom. I’ll check that buttons and links are large enough and easy to select.

## Accessibility checklist

I’ll check that:

- Text is easy to read against its background, including text on buttons and over photos.
- Colour isn’t the only way important information is shown—for example, links and form errors have another visual cue.
- Photos that communicate information have a useful description for people using screen readers; decorative images can be skipped.
- The site can be used with a keyboard, and it’s clear which link or button is currently selected.
- Headings and form labels clearly describe the content or information needed.
- Text can be enlarged, and pages still work on small screens without content being cut off or forcing sideways scrolling.
- Buttons and links are large enough and easy to select.
- Navigation and repeated controls work consistently across pages.
- Form errors explain what needs fixing, and any moving content can be paused or controlled.
